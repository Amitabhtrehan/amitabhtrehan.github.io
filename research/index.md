# Research

My research is broadly in **theoretical computer science and algorithms**, motivated by a recurring question: **how can networked systems compute reliably when the system itself is changing?** This connects distributed computing with graph algorithms, resilience, dynamics and strategic behaviour.

<div class="research-grid">
<article class="research-card">
<h2>Resilient distributed systems</h2>
<p>Algorithms that allow distributed systems to tolerate, repair or reorganise after failures and adversarial changes. This includes self-healing networks, robust network construction and Byzantine settings.</p>
<p class="examples">Representative work: Forgiving Graph, Xheal, DEX and DConstructor.</p>
</article>

<article class="research-card">
<h2>Dynamic networks and information flow</h2>
<p>How information propagates, and how computation proceeds, when network structure or state changes. Current strands include flooding processes, temporal and evolving networks, and distributed estimation.</p>
<p class="examples">Representative work: amnesiac flooding, termination of flooding and dense subgraphs in dynamic networks.</p>
</article>

<article class="research-card">
<h2>Resource-efficient network algorithms</h2>
<p>Distributed algorithms designed under realistic restrictions on memory, messages or local knowledge, including compact routing and efficient network testing.</p>
<p class="examples">Representative work: compact self-healing routing and distributed conductance testing.</p>
</article>

<article class="research-card">
<h2>Coordination, uncertainty and incentives</h2>
<p>Algorithmic questions where agents must coordinate, elect, match or make decisions with incomplete information or strategic incentives.</p>
<p class="examples">Representative work: leader election, stable matching under uncertainty and composition games.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Broader interests

These themes draw on **distributed algorithms, graph algorithms, complexity, game theory and network systems**. They also create connections to peer-to-peer systems, social networks and other engineered or naturally distributed systems.
